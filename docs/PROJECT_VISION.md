# Project Vision

Fathom is an open-source interactive atlas of the world's seas and oceans — a single,
explorable reference for the geography of the maritime world. Its name comes from the old
unit of depth: the measure a crew called out as they sounded unknown waters.

## Mission

Fathom is an interactive maritime atlas. It gathers the things that define the world's
waters — oceans and seas, straits and channels, coastlines and islands, ports and
harbors, routes and boundaries, and the features hidden beneath the surface — and
presents them as one coherent, navigable whole.

It is a reference work in the tradition of the great atlases: curated, considered, and
built for both the quick answer and the long, absorbing detour. Where a printed atlas is
frozen at publication, Fathom is alive — explorable at any scale, layered from a
first glance down to real depth, and continuously corrected and extended in the open.

## Why it exists

The sea covers more than 70% of Earth's surface, carries most of the world's trade,
and has shaped the history, economy, and culture of nearly every coastal civilization
— yet on most maps it is rendered as empty blue space
between the places that matter. The knowledge exists, but it is scattered and hard to
reach: nautical charts are specialist tools built for navigation, not understanding;
encyclopedic sources describe the sea in text, without the geography that gives it
meaning; and general-purpose maps treat water as the end of the map rather than the
beginning of another one.

There is no single, open, welcoming place where anyone can explore and understand the
maritime world. Fathom exists to be that place: to make the geography of the sea as
legible, as browsable, and as engaging as the geography of land has always been.

## Who it is for

- **The curious.** Anyone who has traced a coastline on a map and wondered what that
  strait is called, why that shipping lane bends, or what lies between two islands.
- **Students and educators.** A classroom-ready way to teach geography, trade, history,
  and the physical world — visual, interactive, and free.
- **Sailors and maritime enthusiasts.** People who know the sea and want a beautiful,
  trustworthy reference for the waters they love — while planning nothing more serious
  than their imagination allows.
- **Researchers, journalists, and writers.** A fast, citable first stop for checking a
  name, a location, a route, or a relationship between places.
- **The open-knowledge community.** Contributors who believe reference works of record
  should be open, and who want to help build and maintain one.

## The long-term vision

Fathom aims to become the definitive interactive maritime atlas — the first place anyone
turns to see, check, or understand anything about the world's waters.

Definitive means comprehensive: every ocean, sea, strait, and channel; the ports,
routes, and boundaries that make up the human geography of the sea; and the physical
world beneath it. Definitive also means trusted: carefully sourced, honestly uncertain
where knowledge is uncertain, and quick to correct.

The long-term ambition is for Fathom to hold the same place for the maritime world that
the best reference works hold for theirs — a work of record, freely available to
everyone, maintained by a community that cares about getting it right, and durable
enough to be worth citing for decades.

## Core principles

1. **The sea is the subject, not the background.** Every design and content decision
   starts from the water. Land exists on Fathom's maps to give the sea its shape.
2. **Approachable first, deep second.** A newcomer should understand what they see at
   first glance; an expert should find real substance underneath. Depth is layered, never
   demanded.
3. **Accuracy over volume.** A smaller atlas that is right is worth more than a larger
   one that is almost right. Claims carry sources; uncertainty is stated, not hidden.
4. **Open by default.** The project, its data, and its editorial process are open.
   Anyone can inspect it, correct it, extend it, or build upon it.
5. **Craft matters.** An atlas is a work of cartography, and cartography is a craft.
   Clarity, restraint, and beauty in presentation are features, not decoration.
6. **A reference, not a feed.** Fathom is calm. It exists to answer questions and reward
   curiosity, not to capture attention or manufacture engagement.

## What Fathom is not

- **Not a navigation tool.** Fathom must never be used for real-world navigation or
  passage planning. It is no substitute for official hydrographic charts and
  publications, and it will not pretend to be.
- **Not a vessel tracker.** Fathom describes the enduring geography of the sea, not the
  real-time position of what sails on it.
- **Not a general-purpose map.** The world has excellent street maps. Fathom does not
  compete with them; it covers what they leave blank.
- **Not a raw data repository.** Fathom curates and presents; it is not a substitute for
  the scientific and hydrographic datasets it draws upon and credits.
- **Not a product with an engagement model.** No accounts required to explore, no
  attention economics, no dark patterns. The atlas is the product, and it is free.

## Scope

Fathom will be developed incrementally.

The first public release focuses on the world's major straits.

Future releases will expand to include seas, oceans, channels, canals, islands, ports, shipping routes, maritime boundaries, ocean currents, undersea cables, and other maritime features.

Every expansion must preserve the project's guiding principle: depth, accuracy, and clarity over feature count.

## Success criteria

Fathom is succeeding when:

- **It is the natural first link.** When someone wants to understand a strait, a sea, or
  a route, Fathom is the reference they are sent to — in classrooms, in articles, in
  conversation.
- **Coverage is worthy of the word "atlas."** The named waters of the world are present,
  described, and connected, with no embarrassing blank spots.
- **It is trusted.** Sources are visible, errors are rare, and corrections land quickly.
  Being cited by educators, journalists, and writers is routine.
- **A community sustains it.** Contributions arrive steadily from people the founders
  have never met, and the project can outlive any single maintainer.
- **It stays open and free.** Access never narrows, the data remains open, and the
  project's independence is never traded away.
- **People linger.** The clearest sign of a good atlas: visitors who came for one answer
  and stayed an hour, following the water.

## Open Source Philosophy

Fathom is built in the open.

Every decision, discussion, source, and contribution should be transparent whenever possible.

The project welcomes contributors of many disciplines—not only software engineers, but also cartographers, geographers, historians, educators, designers, translators, photographers, and maritime experts.

The atlas is stronger when knowledge comes from many perspectives.

## Guiding Principle

If forced to choose between adding more content and improving existing content, Fathom will always improve what already exists first.

Quality is cumulative.
Trust is difficult to earn and easy to lose.
